Aldosterone (adrenal cortex, zona glomerulosa): ↑ Na⁺ reabsorption and ↑ K⁺ secretion in the late distal tubule and collecting duct. Cl⁻ and water follow Na⁺.

Vasopressin (posterior pituitary), by contrast: ↑ water reabsorption in the collecting duct.

Thus angiotensin II ↑ salt and water retention and intravascular volume → ↑ systemic BP and renal perfusion pressure

Renin: where it is made and what triggers its release

Afferent arteriole
Where it is made
Juxtaglomerular (granular) cellsin its wall; also described as granular extraglomerular mesangial cells
produce (primarily)
Renin

Renin is a hormone produced primarily by cells located in the wall of the afferent arteriole, the granular extraglomerular mesangial cells, which are specialized juxtaglomerular cells.

What triggers its release
Baroreceptorsin the afferent arteriole, detect ↓ renal perfusion pressure
the decrease stimulates (+)
Renin release

A decrease in renal perfusion pressure, most often caused by systemic hypotension, is detected by baroreceptors in the afferent arteriole and stimulates renin release.

A second sensor
Macula densadetects ↓ NaCl delivery to the distal tubule
Renin release

This is a separate mechanism from the baroreceptors: it responds to the NaCl reaching the distal tubule, not to pressure.
